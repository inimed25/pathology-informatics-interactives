# Debrief

Digital imaging can extend microbiology interpretation beyond the physical laboratory by allowing images to be stored, shared, and reviewed remotely.

However, the reliability of telemicrobiology depends on the entire imaging system. Image capture, resolution, focus, color representation, compression, display quality, and network performance can all influence what the remote reviewer sees.

A technically successful image transfer does not guarantee that the image is adequate for interpretation. Laboratories should validate representative image types and workflows, define when remote review is appropriate, and maintain processes for quality assurance and escalation when image quality is insufficient.

Digital imaging works best when it supports, rather than obscures, the clinical and laboratory context needed for microbiologic interpretation.
