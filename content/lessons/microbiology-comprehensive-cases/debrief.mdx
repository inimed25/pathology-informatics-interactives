# Debrief

Across these cases, technically successful testing or message transmission was never sufficient by itself. Reliable clinical use depended on preserving identity, relationships, meaning, interpretive rules, and intended-use boundaries throughout the workflow.

When approaching a new microbiology informatics problem, use four questions:

1. **What was generated?** Separate the analytical observation from later interpretation.
2. **Where did it travel or change?** Trace instruments, middleware, the LIS, interfaces, the EHR, and external systems.
3. **Was meaning preserved?** Check identifiers, mappings, context, relationships, rules, and downstream display.
4. **What evidence supports action?** Match verification, validation, monitoring, or revalidation to the risk and scope of the change.

> **Capstone takeaway:** Treat the complete information pathway as a governed clinical system.
