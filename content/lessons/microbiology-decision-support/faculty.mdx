# Faculty Guide

## Teaching Focus

This module uses antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) to demonstrate how analytical measurements, interpretive knowledge, rules, and information systems interact.

The central distinction learners should understand is that an AST measurement such as an MIC is not identical to its categorical interpretation.

The module should remain focused on informatics rather than becoming a comprehensive review of antimicrobial susceptibility testing.

## Key Teaching Points

- An MIC is an analytical susceptibility measurement generated under defined testing conditions.
- Breakpoints provide interpretive criteria used to translate susceptibility measurements into categorical interpretations for applicable organism-antimicrobial combinations.
- Because interpretive criteria can change, the same analytical measurement may receive a different categorical interpretation after a breakpoint update.
- Expert systems can apply defined knowledge-based rules to susceptibility data, but automated rules do not eliminate the need for microbiology expertise.
- AST measurements, organisms, antimicrobials, interpretations, comments, and rules may pass through multiple systems before reaching the clinical report.
- A correct analytical measurement can therefore become an incorrect downstream result through outdated criteria, incorrect mappings, inappropriate rules, or interface transformations.
- Antibiograms aggregate susceptibility information across defined collections of isolates and introduce additional data-selection, aggregation, and analysis requirements.
- Breakpoints, expert rules, mappings, and AST configurations require defined ownership, validation, documentation, change control, and ongoing review.

## Suggested Discussion Scenario

An AST instrument generates the expected MIC for an organism-antimicrobial combination, but the EHR displays an unexpected categorical interpretation.

Ask learners:

1. Does the correct MIC establish that the complete AST workflow is functioning correctly?
2. Where in the workflow might the categorical interpretation have been generated?
3. Which breakpoint criteria were applied?
4. Could an expert rule, mapping, or interface transformation have modified the result?
5. What information should be compared across the instrument, middleware, LIS, interface, and EHR?
6. How would the laboratory determine whether a recent configuration or breakpoint change contributed to the discrepancy?

Use the scenario to reinforce that AST troubleshooting should distinguish the **analytical measurement** from the **interpretive and information-processing layers** applied to it.

## Discussion Prompts

- Why is an MIC not equivalent to a susceptible or resistant interpretation?
- What could happen if an instrument and LIS use different breakpoint configurations?
- Which AST rules should require microbiology director oversight?
- How should laboratories approach validation when breakpoints or expert-system configurations change?
- What data-quality problems could affect an antibiogram even when individual AST results are analytically correct?
- How can laboratories verify that susceptibility information displayed in the EHR matches the intended laboratory interpretation?

## Faculty Emphasis

Learners do not need to memorize specific breakpoint values in this module.

Instead, they should understand where breakpoints and expert rules fit within the information pathway and why those configurations require governance.

Avoid reproducing proprietary breakpoint tables or detailed copyrighted standards content in teaching materials. When specific current breakpoints are needed for discussion, consult the current applicable authoritative standard.

The durable troubleshooting framework for this module is:

> **Measurement → interpretation → rules → representation → reporting**

When an AST result appears incorrect, learners should be able to follow that pathway and identify where the discrepancy may have arisen.
