# Faculty Guide

## Teaching Focus

This lesson introduces antimicrobial decision support and expert systems as informatics tools that assist with interpretation of antimicrobial susceptibility data.

## Key Teaching Points

- Expert systems apply predefined rules to antimicrobial susceptibility patterns.
- Decision-support rules can identify unusual resistance phenotypes and support consistent reporting.
- Automated interpretation does not eliminate the need for microbiology expertise and laboratory oversight.
- Rules should have defined ownership and undergo validation before clinical implementation.
- Changes in breakpoints, standards, resistance mechanisms, or laboratory practices may require rule updates.
- Changes to expert systems should be documented, validated, and monitored for downstream effects.

## Discussion Prompts

- What types of susceptibility patterns are useful targets for expert-system rules?
- Who should be responsible for maintaining antimicrobial decision-support rules?
- What should happen when an expert-system interpretation conflicts with laboratory findings?
- How should a laboratory validate a rule after susceptibility standards or breakpoints change?
