# Debrief

When an AST result looks wrong, repeating the analytical test is only one possible investigation.

A useful informatics approach is to trace the result through the complete pathway:

1. **Measurement:** What susceptibility measurement did the AST method or instrument actually generate?
2. **Interpretation:** Which breakpoint criteria were applied, and where was the categorical interpretation generated?
3. **Rules:** Did an expert rule, expected-phenotype rule, reporting policy, or other decision logic affect the result? Where did that rule come from, and where is it implemented?
4. **Representation:** Were the organism, antimicrobial, MIC, category, comments, and associated information represented and mapped correctly?
5. **Reporting:** Did selective reporting, suppression, an interface transformation, or another downstream process change what the clinician ultimately saw?

A correct MIC does not guarantee a correct final report. The analytical measurement may remain unchanged while breakpoints, expert rules, mappings, reporting policies, or downstream configurations change around it.

When a configuration changes, also ask **what depends on it**. A breakpoint update may require coordinated review of the AST system, expert rules, mappings, middleware or LIS configuration, reporting logic, and downstream decision support. Successful implementation in one component does not establish that the complete workflow is correct.

For cumulative data such as an antibiogram, the question changes again. The laboratory must consider whether the underlying population, inclusion rules, duplicate-isolate handling, mappings, and extraction logic support the intended summary.

> **Director-level mental model:** Separate the measurement from its interpretation, identify the rules and systems that act on it, and verify that the intended information survives the pathway to its clinical use. If the pathway is functioning as designed, determine whether the remaining discrepancy reflects analytical uncertainty, interpretive criteria, or a situation requiring microbiologic and clinical judgment rather than an informatics correction.
