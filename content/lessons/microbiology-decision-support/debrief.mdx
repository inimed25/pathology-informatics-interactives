# Debrief

Antimicrobial decision-support systems can help laboratories interpret susceptibility patterns consistently, but their recommendations depend on the rules and data that support them.

Expert rules may identify unusual resistance phenotypes, suppress inappropriate antimicrobial results, or generate interpretive comments. Because these rules can influence the final laboratory report, they should be treated as part of the validated clinical system.

Standards, breakpoints, resistance mechanisms, and laboratory practices change over time. Decision-support rules therefore require defined ownership, validation, version control, and ongoing maintenance.

Automation can support microbiology expertise, but it does not eliminate the need for laboratory oversight. Unexpected patterns and rule conflicts should have clear pathways for review and resolution.
