# Debrief

Public health surveillance depends on more than successfully transmitting laboratory data. The receiving system must also be able to interpret the meaning of the information being exchanged.

Local test names, organism terminology, result formats, specimen descriptions, and reporting conventions may not have the same meaning outside the originating laboratory. Standardized terminology and structured data can help preserve meaning across systems and jurisdictions.

Reporting workflows also require governance. Laboratories must determine which results are reportable, ensure required patient and specimen information accompanies the result, maintain mappings and interfaces, and update workflows as reporting requirements change.

Reliable surveillance therefore depends on both technical interoperability and semantic interoperability: data must reach the correct destination while retaining enough meaning and context to support public health action.
