# Faculty Guide

## Teaching Focus

This lesson introduces clinical microbiology informatics as the integration of laboratory data, information systems, genomic sequencing, bioinformatics, and clinical or public health interpretation.

## Key Teaching Points

- Informatics is broader than software and includes people, data, and workflow.
- Whole-genome sequencing generates data that require validated bioinformatics analysis and interpretation.
- The clinical question should guide the genomic analysis strategy.
- Genomic relatedness should be interpreted alongside epidemiologic and clinical context.
- Successful implementation requires attention to validation, reporting, governance, and data management.

## Discussion Prompts

- Where does informatics already appear in routine microbiology workflows?
- What parts of a WGS workflow require the most informatics support?
- What should a clinical laboratory retain from a genomic sequencing analysis?
- How should laboratories communicate uncertainty in genomic interpretation?
