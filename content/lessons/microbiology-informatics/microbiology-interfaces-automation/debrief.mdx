# Debrief

Automation in clinical microbiology is not simply a matter of replacing manual work with instruments. Automated systems become part of a larger information pathway connecting specimens, instruments, middleware, the LIS, and the clinical record.

Interfaces must preserve the identity and meaning of microbiology data as information moves between systems. Errors in mappings, rules, or system configuration can therefore propagate downstream even when the instrument itself is functioning correctly.

Successful laboratory automation requires validation of both the technical connection and the resulting workflow. Laboratories must consider data integrity, exception handling, system failures, and how automated processes will be monitored and maintained over time.
