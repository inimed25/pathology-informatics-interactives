# Debrief

Microbial genomics is an information workflow, not simply a sequencing technology. The clinical meaning of genomic data depends on how raw sequence information is processed, analyzed, compared, and interpreted.

Different questions require different bioinformatics approaches. Detecting a resistance determinant, identifying a pathogen, and evaluating whether isolates are genetically related are distinct tasks and may require different analytical pipelines, reference databases, thresholds, and validation strategies.

Bioinformatics pipelines used for clinical purposes therefore require validation and ongoing quality oversight. Changes to software, databases, reference genomes, or analytical parameters can alter downstream results even when the underlying sequence data remain unchanged.

Genomic findings must also be interpreted within appropriate clinical and epidemiologic context. Genetic similarity can support an outbreak investigation, for example, but genomic relatedness alone does not establish that transmission occurred between particular patients.
