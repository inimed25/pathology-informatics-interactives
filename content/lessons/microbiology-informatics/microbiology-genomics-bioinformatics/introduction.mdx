# Microbial Genomics and Bioinformatics

Whole-genome sequencing and other next-generation sequencing approaches can provide information beyond traditional organism identification. Sequence data may be used to investigate microbial relatedness, characterize resistance or virulence determinants, support outbreak investigations, and contribute to public health surveillance.

However, sequencing an organism does not by itself produce a clinically meaningful result. Raw sequence data must move through a bioinformatics workflow that may include quality assessment, assembly or alignment, variant detection, annotation, comparison with reference sequences, and interpretation.

The appropriate bioinformatics approach depends on the clinical or epidemiologic question being asked. A workflow designed to identify resistance determinants may differ from one designed to determine whether isolates are genetically related during a suspected outbreak.

In this lesson, you will examine how microbial genomic data move from sequencing through bioinformatics analysis and how analytical choices influence the interpretation of genomic results.
