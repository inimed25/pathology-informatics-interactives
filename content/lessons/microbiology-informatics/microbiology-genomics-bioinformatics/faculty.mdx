# Faculty Guide

## Teaching Focus

This lesson introduces microbial genomics as a clinical informatics workflow. The goal is not to teach learners to become bioinformatics programmers, but to help them understand a sequencing pipeline well enough to evaluate its intended use, major inputs and outputs, validation, limitations, interpretation, and ongoing governance.

Emphasize that **NGS describes a sequencing technology, not a single clinical application or bioinformatics workflow**.

## Key Teaching Points

- Raw sequence data are not themselves a clinical result.
- FASTQ, FASTA, SAM/BAM, and VCF represent different types of information that may appear at different stages of a genomic workflow.
- Quality control, reference alignment, de novo assembly, and downstream analyses should be selected and validated according to the pipeline's intended use.
- Isolate whole-genome sequencing, broad-range targeted sequencing, metagenomic sequencing, and genomic epidemiology answer different questions and create different informatics challenges.
- Broad-range sequencing approaches such as bacterial 16S rRNA or fungal ITS sequencing depend on sequence quality, appropriate reference databases, taxonomic resolution, and validated interpretation criteria.
- Metagenomic NGS can detect microbial nucleic acid within complex specimens, but microbial sequence detection does not automatically establish clinical significance.
- Software versions, reference sequences, databases, algorithms, parameters, and thresholds are components of the analytical workflow and may influence results.
- Pipeline validation should evaluate the computational workflow for its intended use rather than simply demonstrate that sequencing files can be generated.
- Genomic relatedness can support outbreak and surveillance investigations, but genomic similarity alone does not establish direct transmission.
- Changes to software, databases, parameters, or other computational components require appropriate impact assessment, documentation, and change control.

## Discussion Prompts

- How would the informatics workflow differ among isolate whole-genome sequencing, broad-range bacterial 16S rRNA sequencing, and metagenomic sequencing of a clinical specimen?
- Which components of a microbial genomics pipeline should be included when validating the complete workflow?
- How could changing a reference or resistance database alter a result without changing the underlying sequence data?
- If metagenomic sequencing detects sequence assigned to a potential pathogen, what additional information should be considered before determining its clinical significance?
- Why does genomic similarity alone not prove transmission between patients?
- Which parts of a bioinformatics pipeline should a microbiology director understand even if the director does not personally write the software?

## Teaching Principle

> **Follow the sequence data from generation to interpretation. At each step, ask what transformed the information, what could change the result, how that component was validated, and whether the final interpretation remains within the pipeline's intended use.**
