# Microbial Genomics and Bioinformatics

Genomic sequencing can provide detailed information about microorganisms, including organism identification, antimicrobial resistance determinants, genetic relatedness, and potential outbreak relationships.

However, sequencing produces raw data rather than a directly interpretable clinical result. Bioinformatics workflows are required to assess sequence quality, process reads, compare sequences, identify genomic features, and translate those findings into useful information.

The appropriate analytical approach depends on the question being asked. Identifying a resistance gene, characterizing an organism, and investigating whether isolates are genetically related may require different pipelines, databases, parameters, and interpretive thresholds.

In this lesson, you will examine how sequencing data move through a bioinformatics workflow and how validation, quality control, and clinical or epidemiologic context affect genomic interpretation.
