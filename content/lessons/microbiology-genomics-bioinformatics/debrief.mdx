# Debrief

Microbial genomic sequencing produces large amounts of data, but sequence generation alone does not produce a clinically meaningful result.

Bioinformatics pipelines transform raw sequence data through quality assessment, processing, comparison, annotation, and interpretation. The selected pipeline, reference data, software versions, and analytical parameters can influence the conclusions drawn from the same underlying sequence data.

The analytical approach should match the clinical or epidemiologic question. A workflow designed to identify antimicrobial resistance determinants may differ substantially from one used to assess genomic relatedness during an outbreak investigation.

Genomic similarity can support an investigation, but it does not independently prove transmission. Results should be interpreted alongside patient locations, timing, exposures, and other epidemiologic information.
