# Public Health Surveillance and Microbiology Data Exchange

Clinical microbiology laboratories generate information that may be important beyond the care of an individual patient. Organism identification, antimicrobial resistance patterns, molecular results, and genomic data can contribute to public health surveillance and outbreak detection.

For these data to be useful across institutions and jurisdictions, they must be represented in ways that preserve their meaning during exchange. Differences in local test names, organism terminology, result formats, identifiers, and reporting rules can create barriers even when electronic transmission is technically successful.

Public health informatics therefore depends on both interoperability and context. Laboratories must consider which data should be reported, how results are coded, how patient and specimen information are represented, and how reporting requirements are maintained as regulations and surveillance priorities change.

In this lesson, you will examine how clinical microbiology data move from the laboratory into public health systems and identify where informatics problems can interfere with surveillance.
