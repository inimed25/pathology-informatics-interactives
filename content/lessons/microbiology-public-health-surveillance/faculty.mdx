# Faculty Guide

## Teaching Focus

This lesson introduces the informatics role of the clinical microbiology laboratory in public health surveillance and electronic data exchange.

## Key Teaching Points

- Clinical microbiology data can support surveillance, outbreak detection, antimicrobial resistance monitoring, and other public health activities.
- Successful electronic transmission does not guarantee that the receiving system correctly understands the result.
- Structured data and standardized terminology help preserve meaning across different laboratory and public health systems.
- Patient, specimen, organism, test, and result information may all be important to downstream interpretation.
- Reporting requirements and surveillance priorities can change over time, requiring maintenance of interfaces, mappings, and workflows.
- Laboratories share responsibility for ensuring that electronically reported data remain accurate and meaningful.

## Discussion Prompts

- What information should accompany a microbiology result sent to a public health agency?
- How could local organism or test terminology interfere with automated surveillance?
- What is the difference between successfully transmitting a result and successfully communicating its meaning?
- What should happen when public health reporting requirements change?
